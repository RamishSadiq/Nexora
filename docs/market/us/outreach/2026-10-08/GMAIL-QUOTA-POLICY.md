# US campaign Gmail policy

Uses the same verified [quota and backoff policy](../../../outreach/2026-10-08/GMAIL-QUOTA-POLICY.md) as the UK campaign: one active Gmail request, separate recipient messages, 15–18 seconds after a successful send, exponential backoff with jitter up to about 32 seconds, and a bounded retry count. Pacing is a conservative campaign choice, not a mandated Google interval. Check Sent before retrying any uncertain send. Persist each confirmed message ID immediately. No new follow-up automation is created.

The first request with a display-name formatted From header was rejected with HTTP 403 and instructed a Sent check. Sent search found no matching message. Using the authenticated raw sender address, as in the prior UK campaign, succeeded. Subsequent sends use that same authenticated address. No duplicate first pitch was created.

Several later send requests also returned a generic HTTP 403 without a detailed cause; Sent was checked before a bounded delayed retry. A final full-message read returned the detailed underlying rateLimitExceeded response for project 77377267392, with the same 12,000,000 shared project units/minute limit previously observed. The read succeeded after backoff. This supports the shared-quota diagnosis, but does not establish the cause of every generic 403. A transient internal connector error was likewise checked against Sent before retrying. All 30 sends were finally verified, with no duplicate for any recipient and subject.

Suppressions: the global [suppression list](../../../OUTREACH-SUPPRESSIONS.json) is checked before sending. BAFSA is excluded from all future sales contact. A reply of unsubscribe or no thanks stops Nexora marketing to that recipient/organisation. [FTC commercial email guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) applies to B2B outreach; every US pitch identifies the advertisement, states the user-confirmed postal address, and offers reply-based opt-out. Keep the opt-out reply route available for at least 30 days and act within 10 business days, preferably immediately.
